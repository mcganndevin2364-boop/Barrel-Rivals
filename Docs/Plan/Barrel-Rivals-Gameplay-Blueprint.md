**Barrel Rivals — complete-game blueprint**  
September 16, 2026 · Gameplay design revision 3 · Engineering companion revision 4

**The outcome.** A polished iOS/Android arcade barrel-racing game that is enjoyable to replay, gives players horses they want to develop, and supports trustworthy competition. The signature experience is automatic riding from the rider’s first-person bodycam: the player earns performance through a timed launch, remembered drawing patterns, barrel-exit timing and a final sprint challenge. The brief third-person introduction shows the chosen horse and customized rider before entering first person.

The complete product includes live turns and spectating, recorded challenges and simultaneous competition; at least ten distinct horses; earned arena access; horse/gear progression and customization; career/recovery features; coins, diamonds and trophies; rewards, seasons and a tested premium store. Your original eight categories and 53 sections remain the build structure. Exact tuning values below are working recommendations, with decisions and rationale in `Barrel-Rivals-Design-Decisions.md`.

**The reason to return.** A player should leave each race knowing one thing they did well, one skill they could improve, and the next horse/gear/progression goal they are working toward. The main loop is: choose an eligible event → race a rival → understand the result → claim a clear reward → improve a horse/loadout or immediately race again. A stable screen supports collection pride; short missions and occasional Championships add variety to ordinary racing. Keep rewards and menus brief enough that a willing player can return quickly to the arena.

Our working recommendation is **Quick Duel as the main live mode**, with **three-round Championship events** for longer competition. You delegated the format recommendation; shorter commitment and fast rematches are the rationale, not a proven retention result. Test both formats before permanently setting their prominence. Build enjoyment and mastery as the reasons to continue; paid entry pressure, unclear losses and forced delays should not be necessary to sustain a session.

| Mode / format | Rules and purpose | Completion requirement |
|---|---|---|
| Practice | Free, offline-capable learning; retry individual skills or a complete run. | No competitive rewards from unverified offline results; all essential mechanics can be learned here. |
| Quick Duel | Live, one run per rider, waiting rival spectates. Lower penalized time wins. | Fast results/rematch, equivalent challenge schedule, reliable settlement. |
| Championship | Live, three rounds with one run per rider each round; lowest three-run total wins. Show average as well. | Six verified runs, alternating round leaders after a random opening rider, clear standings and forfeit rules. |
| Recorded challenge | Attempt a compatible saved run or a common daily/friend challenge. | Reuse the exact course/challenge/ruleset contract; clearly identify recorded opponents and validate competitive submissions. |
| Simultaneous duel | Two active riders, one run each, separate barrel states and non-colliding horses. | Independent skill windows/clocks, coherent remote presentation, verified results under network delay. |

Quick Duel and Championship are formats of live turn-based play, not separate new networking systems. Avoid dividing a small player population across every tier, format and event at once: feature Quick Duel, schedule Championship events, and use compatible matchmaking pools where rules permit. All requested modes remain part of the full outcome. Low population may lead to a clearly offered recorded challenge, practice, or an explicitly labeled AI training opponent; never silently present a recording/bot as a live account holder.

Initial pacing targets for a normal valid run are roughly 25–35 seconds of real interaction. Aim for about 75–110 seconds from match-found to Quick Duel results, and roughly 3–5 minutes for a Championship. These are prototype budgets including spectator time and short transitions, not measured performance or final promises. Queue time is measured separately. If the skill loop needs more time to feel fair, revise the budgets rather than squeeze unreadable prompts onto the screen.

**A run that communicates cause and effect.**

| Phase | Action and feedback | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Entry and introduction | Select event and legal loadout, meet rival, see a brief horse/rider entrance. | Reserve one entry fee per match if that event charges one. Freeze horse/gear strength and conditions for every run in the match. |
| Gate | Hold a finger through two beeps one second apart; release on the bounded randomized third beep. | Grade release timing Bad/Good/Great/Perfect. The official clock starts at the launch cue. An early release receives a poor launch; a late release includes the delay. A missed release gets a bounded auto-launch grace rule. These outcomes require clear tutorial feedback. |
| Alley | Automatic acceleration towards barrel one. | Launch grade and permitted stats affect acceleration. The player does not manually steer. |
| Preview | Briefly see a recognizable shape; it disappears. | Give both rivals equal target, orientation, preview length and response window at the same course phase. |
| Barrel turn | Draw the remembered shape in a stable screen region during controlled slow motion. | Grade geometric accuracy and completion time; animate the corresponding clean, wide or failed riding line. |
| Exit | Release the drawing touch, then respond to a distinct timing cue. | One exit bonus per barrel; a drawing touch cannot also trigger this bonus. |
| Home sprint | Respond to a small sequence of clear timing beats. | Proposed starting mechanic: three well-spaced beats, not uncontrolled rapid tapping. Tune their timing and benefit through playtests. |
| Results | Show raw race time, individual knock penalties, final time and rival comparison. | One immutable result per run, one settlement per match. Offer a compact replay/skill breakdown and a clear rematch action. |

Each knocked barrel adds **five seconds once**. Early launch mistakes or missed boosts do not also receive an unexplained five-second penalty. A poor drawing can cause a visible contact/knock; its animation and score must originate from the same accepted gameplay event. A second collision callback cannot count it twice. Poor valid play produces a poor time; DNF/forfeit handles failure to complete, invalid runs or abandonment.

**Drawing is the main skill, so the scoring must earn trust.** Begin with curated single-stroke templates such as circle, triangle, square and star. Expand the library through distinct patterns and bounded variations, with tier-specific complexity. Avoid treating trivially rotated copies as unrelated shapes if the recognizer deliberately ignores rotation.

Give each challenge one graded trace, submitted on release or closed at the deadline. Ignore later samples; define cancellation and missing-input outcomes. Normalize screen coordinates, resample consistently, and evaluate coverage, completion and geometric error. Support a reasonable starting point/direction for closed shapes unless the preview explicitly teaches otherwise. Speed earns a benefit only after the trace passes a meaningful shape-quality floor. Partial shapes and rapid scribbles cannot win through speed alone.

A published template matcher such as the [$1 recognizer](https://depts.washington.edu/acelab/proj/dollar/index.html) is a prototype candidate. Recognition of a shape name is different from a reliable competitive accuracy grade; we still need coverage checks, calibration and a varied set of real-phone traces. Test left/right-handed use, finger occlusion, small/large screens, corners, stars, self-intersections and different sampling rates. The drawing region and scoring rules must be stable across visual quality settings.

Keep skill and horse effects separable. Accuracy changes the riding line/recovery and earned boost. Horse agility changes permitted movement/turn performance, not the definition of a correct square. Gear never buys a longer challenge window, a lenient recognizer, advance knowledge of shapes or a smaller barrel penalty. Grade feedback should identify a useful correction without filling the screen with statistics.

**Fair paired challenges.** Store a versioned match manifest containing the course, event rules, round-specific shapes, reveal/response windows, launch timing schedule and environmental conditions. Both riders in each round face that same manifest. Reset footing, weather and barrel state; cosmetic ruts and crowd reactions cannot create a hidden second-rider disadvantage. Randomize the opening rider and alternate who leads each Championship round.

Spectators see the horse, course, timing and completed skill grades. Keep target shapes and raw traces private until both riders finish the round; their own preview remains brief and timed. Protect the network data as well as the UI. This mitigates the in-game memory advantage but cannot prevent external communication or all collusion.

Recorded challenges use the original manifest. Raw-time leaderboards need compatible course, manifest, ruleset version and event class; use common daily challenge sets for broad fastest-time comparisons. Ordinary randomly generated duels can award trophies without pretending their raw times came from identical tests. Replays preserve the applicable version and are not silently re-scored under new balance rules.

**Clock, penalties and live presentation.** Two clocks serve distinct purposes: a trusted real-time deadline for receiving/validating input, and a validated simulation clock for riding time. During each fixed barrel window, the simulation clock advances at the event’s prescribed slow-motion rate. Completing the drawing early earns a later performance benefit; it does not shorten the fixed window or let the player extend it.

For a completed run: `final time = validated simulated riding time from launch cue to legal finish + 5 seconds × knocked barrels`. Use integer time units for storage/comparison, and round only for display. The launch-cue origin is an explicit arcade design choice, separate from a regulation timing-beam convention.

Both riders get identical window lengths and slowdown profiles. For three completed windows this adds the same real-time-versus-simulation-time offset, so timing remains comparable. Test the relationship during actual implementation, including all failure paths. Do not calculate competitive results from client frame rate, local pause, arbitrary `Time.timeScale` changes or an uploaded final stopwatch value.

Network delivery delay is not drawing skill. Establish server schedules, timestamp validation and bounded latency handling before ranking online results. Test delayed/reordered input, audio/visual cue timing and reconnects; local timestamps alone do not prove honesty. A competitive pause cannot extend a deadline. Offline practice may pause.

In simultaneous mode, each rider has independent progression, deadlines, slow-motion windows and barrel state. One rider cannot globally slow the other. Remote motion is presentation against a common network timeline, while the final result comes from each validated run plus penalties. Spectators and players need an honest gap/result display when the visually leading horse has penalties. Validate this mode early before committing to extensive art built around an untested timing model.

**Winner and interruption rules.** Quick Duel compares one penalized time each. Championship compares the sum of three penalized times; dividing both sums by three gives the same winner. Compare the underlying stored values, not rounded HUD text. An exact tie is a draw: refund entry stakes, award no winner prize or trophy transfer, keep already consumed match items consumed, and allow a rematch. DNF is a status, not a zero time. A valid completed competitor beats a DNF. Championship requires three valid runs; a player-caused DNF or forfeit ends that player's attempt without a replacement run. Both player-caused DNFs settle as a double-loss with no winner prize and normal spent stakes/items. Confirmed service failures use the separate void/refund policy.

Reserve the match entry once before play. Consume event consumables once at the published match-start boundary, covering all Championship runs; show this duration before equipping. A cancellation before start releases reservations. A player timeout/forfeit follows the published loss policy; a service-caused void refunds entry and restores reserved/consumed match items through an idempotent correction. Reward calculations must distinguish those cases so disconnecting cannot repeatedly mint refunds. Reconnection does not rewind already accepted skill phases.

**Ten horses and a stable worth returning to.** Use ten distinct entries as the initial full-roster minimum, distributed across the five tiers already sketched in the repository. Start with two complementary horse profiles per tier as a content/balance proposal: for example, stronger acceleration versus more stable turning. Final horse identities, breeds and art require a content pass. Distinguish them through silhouette/markings, animation presentation, personality and bounded performance; coat swaps alone do not fulfill the whole collection goal.

Horse ownership, upgrade level, career state and event eligibility are separate records. A developed starter remains useful in its class; higher classes provide higher performance ceilings and fresh skill difficulty. Unlock the next event through trophies and demonstrated play, with an attainable horse-readiness requirement. Document a complete free route to every competitive class. Buying a premium horse gives ownership/progression value but does not buy earned event access.

Comparable games support meaningful equipment progression: [CSR2](https://www.zynga.com/games/csr-racing-2/) emphasizes upgrades/tuning and collection, and [8 Ball Pool](https://support.miniclip.com/hc/en-us/articles/115015644088-Victory-Cues-8-Ball-Pool) gives upgradeable cues real stats. Our working choice keeps visible horse/gear advantages inside explicit event power limits and matchmaking bands. Publish how an owned horse’s effective stats change for the selected event. The comparison motivates the model; it does not prove our eventual balance or retention.

Balance with concrete tests: identical skill inputs should expose the intended stat difference; a meaningfully cleaner run by the weaker legal loadout should be able to overcome that difference. A premium setup should not erase a normal five-second knock disadvantage against a clean comparable opponent. Exact caps and bonus percentages follow those tests. Purchases and upgrades must never secretly change recognition thresholds or matchmaking to guarantee wins.

**Progression, rewards and recovery.** Coins fund ordinary upgrades and eligible event fees; diamonds fund clearly specified premium offers; trophies are earned progression. Always offer free practice and a free-entry novice competition route that can earn legitimate recovery rewards with anti-farming limits. Check a zero-balance player and a prolonged loss streak explicitly. Required gear charges or horse recovery must not make every playable route disappear.

Use temporary gear as a visible pre-match choice with bounded stacking, declared duration and reliable consumption/refund behavior. Offer horse/gear/cosmetic packs with clear contents, an optional pass and earnable crates. Missions should reward ordinary racing, skill improvement and collection development. Keep a nearby attainable goal and longer trophy-road goals; do not make every screen demand a separate chore.

Career/aging should begin with maturity, experience and milestones. Injury/recovery remains in the full plan, with reversible consequences, preserved ownership and an available route to play. Its exact competitive effects require a prototype before activation; do not silently add irreversible retirement or paid-only recovery. Freeze relevant condition for a complete match so the second run does not inherit an unannounced disadvantage.

For purchases, use verified backend entitlements and tested mobile-store flows. Begin with fixed-content paid packs while progression balance develops. If randomized items are sold, disclose actual odds before purchase under [Apple’s guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/) and [Google Play’s policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9858738?hl=en). Prices, odds, pass duration and final offer contents are open tuning/business decisions; no revenue promise is implied.

**Presentation and content standard.** Horse/rider animation, bodycam framing, hoof cadence, believable turning, dirt contact and readable skill feedback get priority. Lens distortion, chromatic effects, blur, exposure and shake are configurable polish. Keep the drawing surface stable; provide a comfortable reduced-motion profile with identical competitive rules. Render one representative final-quality horse/arena on target phones early, then scale assets using measured budgets and consistent art direction.

Map the five event tiers to five coherent arena presentations; reuse production assets where appropriate while changing composition, identity and atmosphere. The standard course reference is 90 feet between the first two barrel centers, 105 feet from each to the third and a 60-foot score line. Verify layout/clearances and distinguish any shortened arcade variants. [WPRA standard-pattern description](https://wpra.com/the-proof-is-in-the-dirt-in-the-wilderness-circuit/).

**Implementation companion.** `Barrel-Rivals-Engineering-Playbook.md` maps every original section to its stack, specialist work, code/assets, integration and verification. Its shared C# core, Unity adapters and proposed trusted service stack implement the rules here; stack changes must preserve these player-facing contracts.

**Technical ownership.** Keep validated race rules, input grading and time calculation in a versioned simulation layer with tests. Unity handles presentation and input capture; trusted services own eligibility, challenge assignments, verified results, wallet and entitlements. Networking carries session and spectator/remote state. Photon Fusion 2 remains the intended candidate, but mobile topology, authentication, reconnect behavior and cost need a proof before commitment. Photon documents distinct trust and mobile-connectivity tradeoffs by topology. [Photon guidance](https://doc.photonengine.com/fusion/v2/fusion-choose).

Do not assume Unity physics produces identical replay results on every device. Prototype a bounded reproducible movement/scoring model or validate on an authoritative simulation. Macro input, collusion and account abuse remain detection/operation concerns even when payloads and scores are validated. Instrument rejected runs, disagreements and economic corrections with enough context to diagnose them without excessive personal data collection.

**What full completion means.** All three requested competition modes, the ten-horse roster, earned five-tier journey, gear/rewards/store, career/recovery rules, coherent arenas/bodycam/audio/UI, onboarding, safe account recovery, stable backend services and verified iOS/Android builds must pass their mapped acceptance checks. A one-horse prototype is an intermediate proof. Evidence for the complete product includes actual device builds, network-failure tests, economic recovery scenarios, representative new-player sessions and store-readiness records.
